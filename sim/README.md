# /sim: URDF + MuJoCo model of Budget-22

All files here are generated by `cad/sim_export.py` from the same CAD that produces the printable STLs. Do not hand-edit them; change `cad/` and re-export.

| File | What |
|---|---|
| `budget22.xml` | MuJoCo MJCF: 22 position actuators, IMU site + gyro/accelerometer/quat sensors, `head_cam` camera, `stand` keyframe |
| `budget22.urdf` | Same robot as URDF (for Isaac Lab / Isaac Sim / PyBullet / ROS) |
| `meshes/*.obj` | Visual meshes per link in link-local frame, meters |
| `joint_map.csv` | Servo ID, joint name, parent/child, axis, safe limits, nominal limits, shaft location (mm, world) |
| `mass_properties.json` | Per-link mass, COM, inertia, and where each number came from |
| `check_mujoco.py` | Load + stand check |

Units: meters, kilograms, radians. Zero pose = standing, arms down, every servo at mid position (2048 counts). Link frames are world-aligned at the zero pose, with the origin on the joint shaft. Total mass 2.28 kg, COM about (-13, 2, 323) mm.

## Check it

```bash
pip install mujoco numpy
python sim/check_mujoco.py                    # 5 s stand at the zero pose
python sim/check_mujoco.py --kp 30 --kv 0.6   # try other gains
python -m mujoco.viewer --mjcf=sim/budget22.xml
```

What `check_mujoco.py` does: loads the MJCF, resets to the `stand` keyframe (all targets zero), steps 5 s, and reports center-of-mass sag, torso tilt, whether both foot soles are in contact, and actuator load as a fraction of the 2.94 N m servo stall torque. It exits non-zero if sag > 30 mm, tilt > 5 deg, or a foot loses contact.

Result at the committed settings (kp 20, kv 0.5), run on Linux with MuJoCo 3:

```
model: 24 bodies, 23 joints (1 free), 22 actuators, total mass 2.278 kg
initial COM (m): [-0.013  0.001  0.325]
after 5.0 s: COM z 0.322 m (start 0.325, max sag 3.0 mm), COM xy drift 6.0 mm
torso tilt: final 1.87 deg, max 2.36 deg; both feet in contact: True
peak actuator load: l_ankle_pitch 0.10, r_ankle_pitch 0.10, l_knee 0.09 (fraction of stall)
RESULT: PASS - stands (position hold at zero pose)
```

This proves the model loads, the masses and inertias are sane, and a stiff position hold at the zero pose is statically stable on the foot soles. It is **not** a balance controller or a walking result. With the first-guess gains (kp 8, kv 0.35) the torso leaned about 9 deg from servo compliance, so the gains matter. Real STS3215 stiffness depends on its internal P gain; identify yours (step responses) before trusting sim torque numbers.

## Model details and conventions

- Joints: 22 revolute. Names `{r,l}_{hip_yaw,hip_roll,hip_pitch,knee,ankle_pitch,ankle_roll,shoulder_pitch,shoulder_roll,elbow,gripper}` plus `neck_yaw`, `neck_pitch`. Servo IDs: right leg 1 to 6, left leg 7 to 12, right arm 13 to 16, left arm 17 to 20, neck 21 and 22.
- Axes are listed per joint in `joint_map.csv`. Left and right limbs are mirrored in the design, so some left-side axes point the opposite way; **use the axis column and limits per joint, do not assume left = right.** On hardware, map: `servo_counts = 2048 + direction * angle_rad * 4096 / (2 pi)`, with the direction sign for each servo found at bring-up (first-power-on sign check at low torque).
- Actuators: MuJoCo `position` actuators (kp 20 N m/rad, kv 0.5, torque clamp 2.94 N m = STS3215 12 V stall). Joint damping 0.05, armature 0.02 kg m^2, friction loss 0.03 N m. All **starting values**, not identified.
- Velocity limit used for URDF: 4.72 rad/s (STS3215 no-load at 12 V). Rated torque 0.98 N m, stall 2.94 N m.
- Inertia: per link, from the printed STL mesh (PLA 1.24 g/cc x 0.6 fill), the servo mass (55 g + 4 g hardware, as a mesh-shaped body) and electronics estimates, combined with the parallel-axis theorem. `mass_properties.json` lists the parts.
- Contacts: only foot soles collide (box per foot, friction 1.0). **Arms, torso and head have no collision geometry yet, and self-collision is off.** A fall will pass through the floor. Add collision boxes before training anything that falls.
- Sensors: `imu` site in the torso (BNO085 location) with `imu_quat`, `imu_gyro`, `imu_acc`. `head_cam` camera at the head lens hole, 66 deg vertical FOV (assumed; set from your camera module's real FOV).
- Servo actuator model: no backlash, no voltage sag, no communication latency (a shared serial bus with 22 servos is far slower than simulated actuators; measure your real read/write cycle time and match the sim control rate to it).

## Isaac Lab notes

1. Convert the URDF to USD with Isaac Lab's `scripts/tools/convert_urdf.py` (or the UrdfConverter in code), `--fix-base` off, `--joint-stiffness 0 --joint-damping 0` if you drive with your own actuator model. Meshes are OBJ in meters; keep `meshes/` next to the URDF.
2. Use an `ImplicitActuatorCfg` with `effort_limit=2.94`, `velocity_limit=4.72`, `stiffness=20`, `damping=0.5` as the first pass, or an explicit actuator net / DC-motor model (`DCMotorCfg(saturation_effort=2.94, effort_limit=0.98, velocity_limit=4.72)`) once you have measured servo data. Do not just use the stall number as a continuous limit: rated torque is 0.98 N m.
3. Initial state: base height 0.002 m (feet on the floor at the zero pose, verify against the soles' z), all joint positions 0.
4. Add collision geometry for torso/head/arms (not present) and enable self-collisions only after adding them. The URDF only has foot collisions, as the MJCF does.
5. Observation set for the Pi 5 policy: IMU quaternion and gyro (BNO085, 100 Hz), joint positions from servo feedback (rate limited by the bus, measure it), previous action. Joint order in training must match `joint_map.csv` servo IDs.
6. Domain randomization that matters for this hardware: mass +/-15% (printed parts vary), kp +/-30%, friction loss 0.01 to 0.1, control latency 10 to 40 ms, joint zero offset +/-0.05 rad (horn mounting error; calibrate zero in software).
7. Treat the sim as a starting point for sim2real: first validate with the real servos by commanding the same sine joint trajectories on hardware and in sim and fitting kp, damping, armature, frictionloss.
