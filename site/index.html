Budget-22 humanoid

A dirt-cheap but capable 22-DOF biped: about 0.61 m tall, about 2.3 kg (CAD estimate), Feetech ST3215 12 V serial-bus servos, Raspberry Pi 5 brain, ESP32-S3 real-time core. Everything here is an open design: component map, 3D viewer, electrical schematic, BOM, and CAD for 3D printing and sim-to-real.

Status: design study built from published precedents (K2, Open Duck Mini v2, Zeroth-01). Nobody has built this exact 22-DOF robot yet: the printable frame has been checked in software only (watertight meshes, bed fit, collision sweep, a MuJoCo stand test), not printed or assembled, and mass, torque margins and walking speed are estimates. Degrees of freedom and servo IDs

22 servos on two half-duplex 1 Mbps TTL buses. Bus A = legs (IDs 1–12), bus B = arms, grippers, neck (IDs 13–22). Joint names in the simulation files will match these IDs.

The Zeroth lesson: K-Scale found STS3215 too slow for the legs of a ~2.8 kg robot and moved to STS3250. These are not like-for-like. The others are larger, much heavier, and have far stronger actuators and compute (Booster lists 60 N·m peak torque and 48 to 200 TOPS). Ours is a small hobby-servo robot (2.94 N·m stall per servo, datasheet) that has only passed a software stand test. I make no “cheapest” claim until a unit is built and measured. Resellers list some of these robots at much higher prices than the makers (the Booster K1 Geek is listed at $11,998 by one US reseller, Robotics Center).

Path from kit to assembled

  1. Prototype and test (now): print the fit coupons, then one robot; measure real servo torque and walking runs (the sim check is a stand test only).
  2. Kit SKU: printed parts, servos, electronics and the M2/M3 hardware list in a box with the print/assembly guide (the repo already has these). The buyer assembles. Needs a real BOM at volume, shipping and packaging costs, and support.
  3. Assembled SKU: the same kit, assembled, servo IDs set, calibrated and walk-tested. Needs fixtures, labour time per unit, a warranty and returns plan. It also needs checks for the LiPo battery and charger and for radio emissions. I have not assessed those requirements.
  4. Later: injection-molded shells (see DFM notes) only after volume justifies tooling.

Ideas adopted from BRIDGE arXiv 2609.03497

BRIDGE: An Open-Source Humanoid Platform via Morphology-Control Co-Design for Physical AI (Wang, Qian, Wang, Wu, Zong, Gupta, Pathak; CMU, HUST, JoyIn AI). It co-designs the body and the controller for human-like motion. The result is an open-source 88 cm, about 12.5 to 13 kg, 21-DoF humanoid listed at about $1.5K (the paper's Table 1). It uses geared 10, 25 and 55 N·m class actuators and a parallel ankle, and it is tested in MuJoCo. The paper says little about shells, printing or electronics, so nothing is taken from it on those topics.

Idea in the paperStatus hereWhy
Start every joint with the smallest actuator and upgrade only joints that really saturate (failure-guided upgrade)AdoptedThis already matches the plan: STS3215 everywhere, STS3250 only on hip pitch and knees as an upgrade tier. sim/check_mujoco.py now prints “upgrade candidates” (peak load above 0.8). That is a stand test only. Confirm across motions and seeds before buying the $65 servos.
Use experimentally calibrated torque limits, not datasheet peaksAdopted (as a switch)check_mujoco.py --derate reports utilization against the fraction of stall torque you actually measure. The default is still the datasheet 2.94 N·m, so the sim numbers are optimistic until a real servo is measured.
Stack compound joints (hip, shoulder) by offsetting roll and yaw axes along the limb, with spacing at least the half-lengths plus a mounting clearancePartlyThe CAD already offsets the axes because the servos are bulky, but I have not checked our offsets against that inequality. It is listed as to-do.
Rebuild the URDF, meshes and inertias whenever an actuator changesAlready how this repo workscad/sim_export.py regenerates URDF, MJCF, meshes and mass properties from the CAD in one run.
Track human motion data (SONIC, BeyondMimic on LaFAN1, evaluated in MuJoCo) as the controller benchmarkSuggested, not runThe MJCF and URDF can be used as a target for those pipelines. I did not train or run any of them, so there are no results to claim.
One waist DoF (yaw was best in their screening)Not adoptedIt would add a $19.90 servo and a torso redesign. Worth testing later if motion-tracking results need it.
Geared 10/25/55 N·m actuators, parallel ankle, 88 cm and 12.5 kgNot adoptedThat is a different cost class (their listed price is about $1.5K against about $800 here) and a larger robot. This design stays on $20 hobby servos.
Human-likeness metric from retargeting and tracking errorNot adoptedIt needs a motion dataset and trained policies. No retargeting has been done for this robot.
