Budget-19 humanoid

A dirt-cheap but capable 19-DOF biped: about 0.61 m tall, about 2.2 kg (CAD estimate), Feetech STS3215 12 V serial-bus servos (STS3250 on hip pitch and knee), Raspberry Pi 5 brain, ESP32-S3 real-time core. Everything here is an open design: component map, 3D viewer, electrical schematic, BOM, and CAD for 3D printing and sim-to-real.

Status: design study built from published precedents (K2, Open Duck Mini v2, Zeroth-01). Nobody has built this exact 19-DOF robot yet: the printable frame has been checked in software only (watertight meshes, bed fit, collision sweep, a MuJoCo stand test), not printed or assembled, and mass, torque margins and walking speed are estimates. The 3D viewer shows which parts are real vendor CAD, which are modelled from datasheet dimensions and which are generated in this repo. See CAD + sim status and the /cad and /sim folders.

Degrees of freedom and servo IDs

19 servos on two half-duplex 1 Mbps TTL buses. Bus A = legs (IDs 1–12), bus B = arms, grippers, neck (IDs 13–19). Joint names in the simulation files will match these IDs.

Why 19 and not more

Goal: walk, balance and pick up light objects at the lowest cost and part count. These are design judgements. None of them has been validated on hardware, and the sim check is a stand test only.

  • Neck: yaw only. A pitch joint costs a servo ($19.90) and a bracket. The camera can be aimed up and down with the torso and hip pitch, and the head still turns to look around.
  • Arms: shoulder pitch, elbow, gripper. Arms swing in the sagittal plane for balance and reach objects in front of the robot; the legs turn the body to face them. Dropping shoulder roll removes two servos ($39.80) and two parts. It gives up raising an arm sideways and reaching around the body.
  • Legs: six joints each. Hip yaw (turning), hip roll and ankle roll (lateral balance and stepping) stay. They are the next candidates to remove (each saves two servos and two parts), but only after the simulated walking policy shows it still balances without them.
  • Actuators: STS3250 on hip pitch and knee (4), STS3215 on the other 15. Same 45.2 x 24.7 x 35 mm outline and horn, so the printed pods are identical. Hip pitch and knee carry the body weight: in the ready pose single-leg stance already needs about 1 N·m at the knee, and the simulated stand-with-pushes test peaks at 1.0 to 1.45 N·m there, against 2.94 N·m stall for an STS3215 that also drops with speed (Zeroth-01 hit the same limit and moved its legs to STS3250). The STS3250 is 50 kg·cm, 75 rpm, 74.5 g and about $65 against $19.90, so it is used only where the load is. Hip roll (peaks near 1.1 N·m) is the next candidate if it overheats. This is a first-order choice, not a walking test on hardware.
  • Parts merged into structure: the pelvis is part of the torso print, the neck bracket is part of the head, the shoulder link is part of the upper arm, and the covers over the servos are gone because each pod is the bracket.

Build and print guide

Print the two fit coupons first, then the 23 parts (every STL is already in print orientation; the torso needs about 220 mm of Z). Full guide with settings, per-part table, fasteners and assembly order: cad/PRINT_GUIDE.md.

  1. Fit coupons (python cad/fit_coupon.py, under an hour): a real servo must drop into the sleeve coupon and sit on the horn plate coupon. Do this with an STS3250 as well as an STS3215.
  2. Print PLA+ or PETG, 0.4 mm nozzle, 0.2 mm layers, 4 perimeters; PETG for feet, ankle and shin. About 640 g of filament at the estimated fill. One STL per part, left and right are separate files.
  3. Servos: set IDs 1 to 19 one at a time on the bench (joint map), then fit each servo in its pod (4 x M2 self-tapping) and a horn plate on each horn (4 x M3), horns at the mid position.
  4. Torso: 8 M2 heat-set inserts, Pi 5 on its standoffs, battery between the ribs, bus adapters on the front cover, e-stop in the top plate, then the front cover and battery door.
  5. Limbs and head from the foot and hand up; daisy-chain the servos on two buses (legs, upper body).
  6. Bring-up (ESP32 flash, servo identification, per-joint direction and zero, harness test, then policy): deploy/README.md. Use a harness for every early test.
