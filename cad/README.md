# /cad: Budget-19 CAD

Parametric, 3D-printable frame for a ~0.61 m, ~2.2 kg humanoid with 19 STS3215 servos, plus the vendor CAD for the parts that have redistributable models. Everything under `stl/`, `step/`, and `../sim/` is **generated by the Python in this folder**. Change a dimension, rerun, and the STL, STEP, URDF, MJCF, 3D viewer model and mass properties all update together.

> Status: software-verified only. Nothing here has been printed, assembled or run on hardware. Read "What is real" and "Known gaps" before relying on it.

## What is real, generated, or an envelope

| Item | Kind | Source / license |
|---|---|---|
| 15 x Feetech STS3215 servo | **Real vendor CAD** | SO-ARM100 `STEP/SO100/STS3215_03a.step`, Apache-2.0, in `vendor/` |
| 4 x Feetech STS3250 servo (hip pitch, knee) | **Modelled** | Same 45.2 x 24.7 x 35 mm outline as the STS3215 (Feetech datasheet), so the STS3215 STEP is used as its shape. Mass 74.5 g and 4.9 N m stall are in the sim; mounting holes not checked. |
| BNO085 IMU board | **Real vendor CAD** | Adafruit_CAD_Parts `4754 BNO085 STEMMA QT`, MIT, in `vendor/` |
| MAX98357A I2S amp | **Real vendor CAD** | Adafruit_CAD_Parts `3006 MAX98357`, MIT, in `vendor/` |
| 40 mm speaker | **Real vendor CAD** | Adafruit_CAD_Parts `3968 Speaker 40mm`, MIT, in `vendor/` |
| Raspberry Pi 5 | **Real vendor CAD** (decimated visual mesh only in the viewer) | Raspberry Pi product portal STEP, MIT, fetched by `fetch_vendor.py` (77 MB, not stored here) |
| 23 printed parts in 14 unique designs (pods that are the brackets, torso with pelvis, head with neck bracket, plus torso front cover, battery door, head lid) | **Generated** (CadQuery, this repo) | Designed here around the STS3215 STEP outline. Not verified against a physical servo yet. |
| Pi Camera Module 3, XIAO ESP32-S3, Pololu D24V50F5 buck | **Envelope** (size-only box) | Vendor STEPs exist but their license is not clear, so they are neither committed nor shown as CAD. Dimensions only. |
| 3S LiPo, Waveshare bus adapters, I2S mic, e-stop, relay/fuse | **Envelope** | Assumed sizes, flagged in the viewer. Check your actual parts. |

Envelopes are drawn translucent grey-dashed in the viewer and are never labelled as CAD.

## Layout

```
cad/
  lib.py            STS3215 frame constants, sleeve and horn-plate builders (CadQuery)
  robot.py          the 23 parts: joints, links, torso (with pelvis, Pi 5 standoffs, battery bay, e-stop hole, cover bosses), head (with neck bracket), and three covers
  assembly.py       builds parts, servo placements, joint table
  electronics.py    electronics placement (real meshes + envelopes) and overlap check
  collider.py, tune.py, sweep.py   FCL collision checks and joint-limit tuning -> limits.json
  export.py         STEP + STL per part, print analysis, mass properties
  sim_export.py     URDF, MJCF, OBJ meshes, joint_map.csv, glTF for the website
  fit_coupon.py     two small print-fit test parts (print these first)
  vendor/           redistributable vendor STEP files + their licenses
  fetch_vendor.py   downloads the non-redistributed vendor CAD into vendor_fetched/ (git-ignored)
  vendor_meshes.py  decimates vendor STEP into light visual meshes (.cache/, git-ignored)
  stl/ step/        outputs, one file per part
  print_report.json per-part print analysis; PRINT_GUIDE.md human-readable
```

## Rebuild

```bash
python -m venv .venv && source .venv/bin/activate
pip install cadquery trimesh numpy python-fcl rtree fast-simplification mujoco
python cad/fetch_vendor.py            # optional: Pi 5 STEP for the real Pi mesh (add --all for the envelope-source STEPs)
python cad/vendor_meshes.py           # decimate vendor STEP -> cad/.cache (a few minutes with the Pi 5)
python cad/tune.py                    # optional: re-derive safe joint limits (about 1 min), writes cad/limits.json
python cad/sim_export.py              # STEP, STL, print report, URDF, MJCF, joint map, GLB data
python sim/check_mujoco.py            # load + stand check
```

Tested with CadQuery 2.8, Python 3.11 on Linux.

## v4 look (limb shells, sculpted feet, character head)

Same 19 servos, same joint axes, same 23 parts in 14 unique designs (no cover or clamshell parts were added). Changes: the pods are the structure and now run up to the hub, and every joint hub is a pod-sized cover plate (graphite in the viewer) so servo bodies and horns are hidden except the horn screws; the thigh and shin carry closed tapered hollow bulges (shin printed crest-down, thigh plate-down) over their strips; the upper arm carries a tapered bulge behind the elbow plate; the feet are hollow sneaker-style toe and heel shells that stay below the four horn screws; the neck pod and its cable notch are hidden inside a collar on the torso top (cables leave through a slot in the torso top plate); the head gets a brow hood, vent slots, ring grooves round the ear discs, an orange lid with a radar dome and an antenna stub. Cable-tie slots through the thigh and shin and the 2.4 mm flanges were removed because they would open into the hollow bulges. Not printed or fit-tested.

**Honest limits of the shell idea.** A one-piece shell per segment (thigh, shin, upper arm, forearm) that fully swallows its servos was not achieved. The pod-and-hub construction is still the structure, so the servo cases still read as rounded boxes with a bulge fairing beside them; the forearm has no bulge (its strip sits against the thigh during arm swing) and the shoulder strip and hip cluster are unchanged plates. A real clamshell would need extra parts (a cover per segment, about 8 more printed parts and 16 more screws) and would stretch the 110 mm leg spacing; that is the tradeoff if you want it.

## What was checked (all in software)

- All 23 parts build as one valid solid each and every STL is watertight (ASCII, 3-decimal coordinates).
- Every part fits a 256 mm bed in its print orientation (largest: torso 80 x 186 mm footprint, 223.7 mm tall; foot 122 x 63 mm; the 220 mm bed also works for everything except the 224 mm tall torso). Overhang area is at most 10.3% of surface per part (torso), then 8.9% (forearm) and 8.4% (upper arm), so slice-preview the torso (floor over the pelvis skirt), the forearm, the upper-arm bulge and the foot roofs (20 to 25 degrees from horizontal, shallow bridged skins; use a brim or 1 support if the first layers sag).
- Wall thickness (1st percentile of sampled flat-face points): 1.48 mm (head) to 3.0 mm depending on the part, see `print_report.json`; designed walls are 2.0 to 2.4 mm. The `min` estimate in `print_report.json` is about 0 on some parts because the ray test hits coplanar faces at fillet tips and mating planes; a separate 1.2 mm morphological-opening check on 40 slices per axis (head, thigh, shin, upper arm, forearm, foot) found only small tip and fillet areas, no thin sheets (largest 28 mm2, at the upper-arm tip). Slice-preview the bulge tips and the head brow.
- Zero pose has no collisions (FCL, servo + printed parts + electronics). Each joint's safe range is the largest range with no collision with every other joint at zero. **Combined multi-joint motion is not swept.**
- Electronics overlaps: touching contacts only (0.04 mm or less) between bus adapters, torso, BNO085 and the front cover. The covers are included in the check.
- MuJoCo load and stand check passes (see `../sim/README.md`).

## Known gaps

1. **Not printed, not fit-tested.** Horn pattern, case-screw and cable-slot positions come from the STEP, not a physical servo. Print `fit_coupon_*` first.
2. **STS3250 on hip pitch and knee (4 servos).** Chosen from first-order torque estimates (single-leg stance about 0.95 N m at the knee in the ready pose, stand-with-push peaks 1.0 to 1.45 N m at hip pitch and knee in sim) and the K-Scale Zeroth-01 experience, not from a walking test on hardware. The STS3250 has the same outline, so the pods are unchanged, but its mounting holes were not checked. Hip roll peaks near 1.1 N m in the push test and is the next candidate if it overheats.
3. **Masses are estimates** (PLA 1.24 g/cc x 0.6 fill, servo 55 g + 4 g hardware, electronics estimated). Weigh the real parts.
4. **Sim actuator gains** (kp 20, kv 0.5, damping 0.05, armature 0.02, frictionloss 0.03) are starting values, not identified from hardware.
5. **Joint limits** are conservative and single-joint only. Hip yaw and roll are narrow by design (legs 110 mm apart; the pod-sized hub covers cut hip roll to about -0.24/+0.20 rad and hip pitch to -0.6/+0.95 rad on the right leg (mirrored on the left)); arms cannot adduct past the torso.
6. **Envelopes** (camera, XIAO, buck, battery, bus adapters, mic, e-stop) are sizes only. Replace them with real CAD once you have licensed models.
7. **Cable routing is only notches and one slot**: servo-tail notches on the pods (partly covered by the next link), a cable slot in the torso top plate under the neck collar, and 3.2 x 1.6 mm cable-tie slots in the torso side walls only (none on the limbs). There are no enclosed cable channels or strain-relief clips yet.
8. **No bearings opposite the horn.** The STS3215 STEP has only a ~2 mm, Ø6 rear stub with a Ø2.4 hole, so a bearing there is not physically supported. It needs a fork bracket and a check on a physical servo. Not designed.
9. **Servo pods are closed.** Each servo sits in a rounded printed pod that is also the bracket; the limb bulges are fairings on the strips, not separate limb shells. Servos slide in from the horn side and the child part's hub disc closes the pod, so no servo window is cut.
10. **Bus adapters and the Pololu buck are unmounted envelopes.** The Pi 5 (4 standoffs, M2 self-tapping), the XIAO (friction cradle on the front cover, no holes on the board) and the battery (two ribs and two straps) have mounts. The e-stop and XT60 charge port are cut into the printed parts but are envelopes (not CAD).
11. Covers and battery door are fit-checked against the envelopes only. Heat-set insert pockets are 3.2 mm and need a test print.
12. Parts have no license file yet: add one that suits how you want to share the generated frame.
